Worksheet Exercise 5. (4 points).

Let \(C\) be the curve in which the plane \(x + z = 3\) cuts the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 4\text{,}\) traversed counterclockwise when seen from above. Evaluate
\begin{equation*} \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} \qquad\text{for}\qquad \mathbf{F} = \langle z,\ 0,\ x \rangle . \end{equation*}
🔗

Solution.

The quickest route is Stokes’ theorem.
🔗
The curl.
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} \nabla \times \mathbf{F} \amp = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} \amp \mathbf{j} \amp \mathbf{k}\\ \dfrac{\partial}{\partial x} \amp \dfrac{\partial}{\partial y} \amp \dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\\ z \amp 0 \amp x \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp = (0 - 0)\,\mathbf{i} - (1 - 1)\,\mathbf{j}\\ \amp \qquad + (0 - 0)\,\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0} . \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
🔗
The surface. Let \(S\) be the part of the plane \(x + z = 3\) inside the cylinder (Figure 4.14.5), oriented by its upward unit normal \(\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \dfrac{\langle 1,\ 0,\ 1 \rangle}{\sqrt2}\text{;}\) its boundary is \(C\text{,}\) and the upward normal matches the counterclockwise direction on \(C\) by the right-hand rule.
🔗
The integral. By Stokes’ theorem,
\begin{align*} \amp \oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r} = \iint_S (\nabla \times \mathbf{F}) \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}\,d\sigma\\ \amp \quad = \iint_S \mathbf{0} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}\,d\sigma = 0 . \end{align*}
Since the curl vanishes identically, neither the normal nor \(d\sigma\) has to be computed, and any surface with boundary \(C\) gives the same answer.
🔗
Other methods. The problem does not prescribe a method, and two others are equally correct. (i) \(\mathbf{F} = \langle z, 0, x \rangle = \nabla(xz)\) is conservative, so its line integral around any closed curve is \(0\text{.}\) (ii) Directly: with \(\mathbf{r}(t) = \langle 2\cos t,\ 2\sin t,\ 3 - 2\cos t \rangle\text{,}\) \(0 \le t \le 2\pi\text{,}\) we get \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t)) \cdot \mathbf{r}'(t) = -6\sin t + 8\sin t\cos t\text{,}\) whose integral over \([0, 2\pi]\) is \(0\text{.}\)
