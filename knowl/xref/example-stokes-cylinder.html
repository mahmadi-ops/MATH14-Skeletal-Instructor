Example 3.2.10. A Circulation That Is Easier as a Flux.

Evaluate \(\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\) for \(\mathbf{F} = -y^2\,\mathbf{i} + x\,\mathbf{j} + z^2\,\mathbf{k}\text{,}\) where \(C\) is the curve in which the plane \(y + z = 2\) cuts the cylinder \(x^2 + y^2 = 1\text{,}\) traversed counterclockwise when seen from above. The curve is drawn in Figure 3.2.11.
🔗
Solution. (Parametrizing \(C\) directly is possible but unpleasant. Instead take \(S\) to be the flat region that \(C\) bounds in the plane, the graph of \(z = 2 - y\) over the unit disk \(R\text{,}\) shown in Figure 3.2.11. For a graph \(z = g(x,y)\) with an upward normal, (2.10.6) gives \(\hat{\mathbf n}\,d\sigma = \langle -g_x, -g_y, 1\rangle\,dx\,dy\text{;}\) check that upward is what the right-hand rule pairs with a counterclockwise \(C\text{.}\) Compute the curl, dot, and integrate over the disk; one term vanishes by symmetry.)
