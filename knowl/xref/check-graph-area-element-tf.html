Checkpoint 2.10.18. True or False: The Surface Area Element of a Graph.

    Let \(S\) be the graph of a function \(z = f(x,y)\) with continuous first partial derivatives, lying over a region \(D\) of the \(xy\)-plane. Then the area element of \(S\) and the area element \(dA = dx\,dy\) of \(D\) are related by
    \begin{equation*} d\sigma = \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2}\; dA, \end{equation*}
    so that
    \begin{align*} \amp \iint_S g\, d\sigma\\ \amp \quad = \iint_D g\bigl(x, y, f(x,y)\bigr) \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2}\; dA. \end{align*}
    🔗

  • True.

  • True. This is not a new formula to memorize separately. It is the general formula \(d\sigma = \lVert \mathbf{r}_u \times \mathbf{r}_v \rVert\, du\, dv\) for a parametrized surface \(\mathbf{r}(u,v)\text{,}\) from Remark 2.10.11 and (2.10.2), applied to the simplest parametrization a graph has.
    A point of \(S\) is determined by the point \((x,y)\) of \(D\) below it, so we may use \(x\) and \(y\) themselves as the two parameters, as for the paraboloid in Example 2.10.14:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{r}(x,y) = \langle x,\ y,\ f(x,y) \rangle, \qquad (x,y) \in D. \end{equation*}
    Differentiate with respect to each parameter, holding the other fixed:
    \begin{equation*} \mathbf{r}_x = \langle 1,\ 0,\ f_x \rangle, \qquad \mathbf{r}_y = \langle 0,\ 1,\ f_y \rangle. \end{equation*}
    By the determinant formula for the cross product,
    \begin{align*} \mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y \amp = \begin{vmatrix} \mathbf{i} \amp \mathbf{j} \amp \mathbf{k} \\ 1 \amp 0 \amp f_x \\ 0 \amp 1 \amp f_y \end{vmatrix}\\ \amp = \langle -f_x,\ -f_y,\ 1 \rangle, \end{align*}
    and the length of this vector is
    \begin{align*} \lVert \mathbf{r}_x \times \mathbf{r}_y \rVert \amp = \sqrt{(-f_x)^2 + (-f_y)^2 + 1^2}\\ \amp = \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2}. \end{align*}
    With \(u = x\) and \(v = y\) the general formula therefore reads
    \begin{align*} d\sigma \amp = \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2}\; dx\, dy\\ \amp = \sqrt{1 + f_x^2 + f_y^2}\; dA, \end{align*}
    which is the claim.
