Example 3.2.14. A Tilted Circle.

Evaluate \(\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\) for
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} = \ln\left(1 + x^8\right)\mathbf{i} - z\,\mathbf{j} + y\,\mathbf{k}, \end{equation*}
where \(C\) is the intersection of the sphere \(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 9\) with the plane \(z = x\text{,}\) traversed counterclockwise when seen from above.
🔗
Solution. (Where is the centre of the sphere relative to the plane, and so what kind of circle is \(C\text{,}\) with what radius? The \(\ln(1+x^8)\) again rules out a direct attack and again dies under the curl. Take \(S\) to be the flat disk that \(C\) bounds in the plane \(z = x\text{,}\) shown in Figure 3.2.15; a normal to the plane is the gradient of \(z - x\text{,}\) and the right-hand rule picks the upward one. The integrand is constant, so the flux is that constant times the area of the disk. As a check, redo it with the graph protocol \(\hat{\mathbf n}\,d\sigma = \langle -f_x, -f_y, 1\rangle\,dx\,dy\) over the shadow of \(S\text{,}\) which is an ellipse.)
