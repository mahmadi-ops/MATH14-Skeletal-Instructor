Example 1.4.10. Work done by an inverse-square field along a helix.

Calculate the line integral
\begin{align*} \int_C \amp \frac{-x}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dx\\ \amp +\frac{-y}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dy\\ \amp +\frac{-z}{\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{3/2}}\,dz \end{align*}
over the curve \(\mathbf{r}(t)=\langle\cos t,\ \sin t,\ t\rangle\) from \((1,0,0)\) to \((1,0,2\pi)\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution. (This integral is in the differential form (1.4.9): identify \(\mathbf{F} = \langle M, N, P\rangle\) and recognize it as the inverse-square field (1.4.2) of Figure 1.4.3. Read off the parameter interval from the endpoints. Along the helix, simplify \(x^2+y^2+z^2\) first, then write \(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}(t))\) and \(\mathbf{v}(t)\text{;}\) the dot product collapses. Integrate with the substitution \(u = 1 + t^2\text{.}\) Say what the sign of the answer means physically, and check it against Figure 1.4.11 and Figure 1.4.12.)
