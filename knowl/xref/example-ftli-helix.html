Example 1.5.9. Example I.

Consider the scalar-valued function \(f(x,y,z) = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2 + y^2 + z^2}}\text{.}\)
🔗
  1. Calculate the gradient vector field \(\mathbf F(x,y,z) = \nabla f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Evaluate the line integral of the vector field \(\mathbf F(x,y,z) = \nabla f\) over the curve \(C_1\) parametrized as \(\mathbf r(t) = \cos(t)\mathbf i + \sin(t)\mathbf j + t\mathbf k\) from \((1,0,0)\) to \((1,0,2\pi)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. What is the result of \(\int_{C_2} \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\) from \((1,0,0)\) to \((1,0,2\pi)\) where \(C_2\) is the line connecting the two points?
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
Solution. (In A, write \(f = \left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right)^{-1/2}\) and take the three partial derivatives; you should recognize the field. In B, one way would be to parametrize, exactly as in Example 1.4.10; instead use Theorem 1.5.6, which needs only the values of \(f\) at the two endpoints. In C, say what Theorem 1.5.6 tells you about \(C_2\) without computing anything, and why. The two curves are shown in Figure 1.5.10.)
