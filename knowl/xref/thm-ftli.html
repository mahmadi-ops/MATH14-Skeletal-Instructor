Theorem 1.5.6. Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals.

Let \(C\) be a smooth curve joining the point \(A\) to the point \(B\) in the plane or in space and parametrized by \(\mathbf r(t)\text{.}\) Let \(f\) be a differentiable function with a continuous gradient vector \(\mathbf F = \nabla f\) on a domain \(D\) containing \(C\text{.}\) Then
🔗
\begin{equation} \int_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = f(B) - f(A).\tag{1.5.2} \end{equation}
🔗

Proof.

(Parametrize \(C\) by \(\mathbf r(t) = x(t)\mathbf i + y(t)\mathbf j + z(t)\mathbf k\text{,}\) \(a \le t \le b\text{,}\) and write the line integral in the parametrized form (1.4.8) with \(\mathbf F = \nabla f\text{.}\) Expand the dot product of the gradient with \(d\mathbf r/dt\text{;}\) the chain rule turns the integrand into a single derivative with respect to \(t\text{,}\) and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus finishes the job.)
