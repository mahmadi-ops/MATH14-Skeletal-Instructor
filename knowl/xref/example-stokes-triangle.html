Example 3.2.16. Around a Triangle, Twice.

Evaluate \(\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{r}\) for
\begin{equation*} \mathbf{F} = (y - z)\,\mathbf{i} + (x + z)\,\mathbf{j} + \left(x^2 + y\right)\mathbf{k}, \end{equation*}
where \(C\) goes from \((2,0,0)\) to \((0,2,0)\) to \((0,0,4)\) and back to \((2,0,0)\) along straight line segments, as in Figure 3.2.17. Do the computation twice: directly, and by Stokes’ theorem.
🔗
Solution. (Directly: parametrize each of the three segments on \(0 \le t \le 1\text{,}\) compute \(\mathbf F(\mathbf r(t))\cdot\mathbf r'(t)\text{,}\) integrate, and add. By Stokes’ theorem: compute the curl by (3.2.1); the triangle lies in the plane through the three vertices, which is the graph of some \(z = 4 - 2x - 2y\) over a shadow triangle \(R\) in the \(xy\)-plane. Decide which normal the given traversal pairs with, write \(\hat{\mathbf n}\,d\sigma\) for the graph, dot with the curl, and integrate over \(R\text{.}\) Compare the two answers; see Figure 3.2.17.)
