Theorem 3.1.12. Green’s Theorem (Circulation-Curl or Tangential Form).

Let \(C\) be a piecewise smooth, simple closed curve enclosing a region \(R\) in the plane. Let \(\mathbf{F} = M\,\mathbf{i} + N\,\mathbf{j}\) be a vector field with \(M\) and \(N\) having continuous first partial derivatives in an open region containing \(R\text{.}\) Then the counterclockwise circulation of \(\mathbf{F}\) around \(C\) equals the double integral of \((\operatorname{curl} \mathbf{F}) \cdot \mathbf{k}\) over \(R\text{:}\)
🔗
\begin{equation} \underbrace{\oint_C \mathbf{F} \cdot \mathbf{T}\, ds = \oint_C M\, dx + N\, dy}_{\text{counterclockwise circulation}} = \underbrace{\iint_R \left( \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} \right) dx\, dy}_{\text{double integral of curl} \,\cdot\, \mathbf{k} \text{ over } R}.\tag{3.1.5} \end{equation}
🔗

Proof.

(Both sides of (3.1.5) split into a piece carrying \(M\) and a piece carrying \(N\text{,}\) so it is enough to prove the two statements
\begin{align} \oint_C M\,dx \amp = -\iint_R \frac{\partial M}{\partial y}\,dA ,\tag{3.1.6}\\ \oint_C N\,dy \amp = \iint_R \frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\,dA ,\notag \end{align}
and add them. Step 1: for a region of the kind in Subsection 2.2.1, shown in Figure 3.1.13, say why the vertical sides contribute nothing to \(\oint_C M\,dx\text{,}\) write the bottom and top contributions as integrals in \(x\) (mind the direction of travel along the top), and compare with the double integral done in the \(y\)-direction first, where the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus evaluates the inner integral. Step 2: read \(R\) the other way, as in Subsection 2.2.2, and repeat for \(N\text{;}\) say why no minus sign appears this time. Step 3: say what to do with a region that cannot be described both ways, and why the cuts cancel; Figure 3.1.14 shows the idea. Finally, explain how Theorem 3.1.11 follows by applying the tangential form to the field \(\langle -N, M\rangle\text{.}\))
