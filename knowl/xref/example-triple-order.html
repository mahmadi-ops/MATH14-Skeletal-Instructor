Example 2.5.5. Example II.

Set up the triple integrals below whose value gives the volume of the region \(R\) of Figure 2.5.6, bounded by the cylinder \(z = 1 - x^2\text{,}\) the plane \(y = 1 - x\text{,}\) and the coordinate planes. Then, evaluate two out of the three integrals.

    🔗
    🔗

Question: How many more triple integrals can you think of for computing such a volume? (Three more; six in total.)
🔗
Solution. (For each order, the two outer variables describe a projection of \(R\) into a coordinate plane, and the 3D figure gives the limits of the innermost variable. Sketch the three projections on Figure 2.5.7. In A, project into the \(xy\)-plane. In B, project into the \(xz\)-plane; solve the cylinder for \(x\text{.}\) In C, project into the \(yz\)-plane: which surface does a ray in the \(x\)-direction reach first, the plane or the cylinder? The answer depends on where \((y,z)\) is, so the projection splits along a curve, and two integrals are needed. Evaluate two of the three, and check that they agree.)
