Theorem 1.5.16. Loop Property of Conservative Fields.

The following statements are equivalent.
🔗
  1. \(\oint_C \mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r = 0\) around every loop (that is, closed curve \(C\)) in \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The field \(\mathbf F\) is conservative on \(D\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗
🔗

Proof.

((2) implies (1): split any loop as \(C = C_1 \cup C_2\text{,}\) with \(C_1\) from \(A\) to \(B\) and \(C_2\) from \(B\) back to \(A\text{,}\) as in Figure 1.5.17. Reversing \(C_2\) gives a second path \(-C_2\) from \(A\) to \(B\text{;}\) what does path independence say about \(\int_{C_1}\) and \(\int_{-C_2}\text{,}\) and how is \(\int_{-C_2}\) related to \(\int_{C_2}\text{?}\) Add up. (1) implies (2): take any two paths \(C_1\text{,}\) \(C_2\) from \(A\) to \(B\text{,}\) build a loop out of them, and apply the assumption to it.)
